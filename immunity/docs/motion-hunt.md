# Motion Hunt and expedition menu

## Accepted outcome — 2026-10-20

User requested implementation of two selectable modes. Move the survivor into a choice and remain there for less than one second, or press Enter to confirm immediately. The second mode requires collecting a resource essential to humanity’s survival on this planet and then exiting. Existing Survival remains available.

## Experience and implementation decisions

Reuse the selected tilted Glass Tide art and organic interface. Builder owns experience review. The local design mockup is `outputs/mode-menu-mockup.svg`: two clearings, a survivor starting between them, readable mode descriptions, and visible dwell progress. Confirmation takes 850 ms of continuous occupancy. Leaving a clearing resets progress. Keyboard, pointer/touch movement and accessible mode buttons share the same selection. Name entry follows selection; leaving a game returns to mode selection.

First playable mechanics: one life-support seed in the central clear corridor; each player collects their own seed by reaching it. Carry it to any of the four gate thresholds to extract. No seed means no extraction. Capture loses the seed and returns the player outside. Stationary humans provide no target signal; bots halt when no moving target is sensed. Physical contact remains dangerous, including when another human draws a bot past a stationary player. Pursuit uses the existing evolving neural controller behind this explicit sensory rule, without claiming detection itself or improved hunting has been experimentally learned.

Keep Survival’s existing database row, genomes, records and legacy protocol. Motion Hunt uses a separate arena row and mode-specific anonymous score identity, with successful extraction encoded separately from captures. No schema reset or live migration. Reuse shared movement and visible-contact validation. No deployment in this implementation task.

Successful extraction is stored as `bot = -1`; fastest success uses MIN over those rows, excluding failed missions. Both outcomes use the existing atomic world/score transaction and life increment. The 11-input network remains compatible: filter detectable targets before building inputs; halt without activation if none remain. The private issued contact frame records whether this player was moving, so a later stop packet cannot remove a moving-target capture reward. This is sampled observation, not continuous historical motion reconstruction. ## Experimental history controller — 2026-10-24

Accepted user request: implement short-history movement detection, commit and publish it, and reset only Motion Hunt generations for a fresh start. This section supersedes the original fixed-detection behavior above. Survival, player records, collection/extraction, collision/contact validation, artwork and the hosting identity are preserved. Builder review; single agent.

Acceptance: controller decisions depend on temporal positions rather than player velocity or a movement flag; stationary players remain observable; attention selects across every active player; reset persists once under D1 compare-and-swap; subsequent visits retain training; old Motion Hunt archives cannot overwrite the new controller; Survival and saved records remain intact. Checkpoints: domain/format checks, disposable HTTP/D1 and browser review, exact-source build, bounded commit and publication.

Implemented `motion-history-v1`: shared private per-life histories retain at most 12 samples over 300 ms. Each bot evaluates each player using 11 inputs: current relative position (2), two past positions centered on the latest observed player position and scaled by 100 (4), available-history fraction (1), nearest-obstacle direction/clearance (3), bias (1). Current relative position is scaled by 800. Past observations are selected at or before 100/200 ms ago. Every past sample uses world positions, so ego-motion cannot create a false displacement. Missing history is zero-filled with coverage explicitly supplied. No target velocities, speed, detectable flag or fixed nearest-moving-player choice enter activation. Three tanh outputs provide steering x/y and attention; highest positive attention selects a player, and no positive attention selects rest. A fresh random population is used, with no injected detector or pretrained weights.

The evaluator alone retains the >8 world-units/second ground-truth definition. For candidates with full histories, attention receives `2 * dt * (1 - (attention - label)^2)`, averaged across candidates, with labels +1 moving / -1 stationary. These are training labels, not sensor inputs. The selected moving target retains closing/proximity rewards; selecting a stationary target costs `4 * dt * (0.5 + realizedSpeed/290)`. All movement costs `0.1 * dt * realizedSpeed/290`, obstacle contact costs 3/second, moving captures give +50 and stationary captures −25. Existing private issued-frame motion observations determine delayed capture rewards; old-generation reports cannot train the new generation. Thus perception/steering evolve against an explicitly designed objective. Stillness is no longer guaranteed protection.

Controller-specific archives use rules `immunity-motion-history-v1` and three outputs. Survival retains its existing rules and two-output genome representation. Motion Hunt's first read after publication replaces its old bot population, evolution state, generation/history/best/champion and queued imports with a fresh generation 1; clears old issued contact frames; retains terrain, active player lives/cargo/records and score rows. The upgrade marker is persisted with a revision check and does not repeat. Incompatible stored checkpoint/rollback files are unavailable through exports; new compatible checkpoints replace the slots normally. No schema migration, score deletion or Survival reset is required.

Verification in a disposable checkout/database at `/private/tmp/immunity-motion-work/immunity`: 104 tests and TypeScript pass; production build passes; all five sequential HTTP/D1 fixtures pass. New checks include position-only activation independent of supplied velocity, learned attention/rest without a moving-only filter, stationary pursuit penalty, ego-motion invariance, bounded/life-scoped histories, one-time reset and Survival preservation, three-output archive evolution/validation, HTTP-persisted reset, mission/contact/record/rejoin behavior. Browser verification and final release evidence follow.

Offline feasibility trial: `npm test` then `node scripts/motion-history-trial.mjs`. Three seeds, 24 genomes, 200 generations, 128 balanced synthetic training examples and 256 separate balanced holdout examples per seed. Same observation builder and evolutionary operators; detector squared-error objective only, with no arena pursuit, obstacles, network jitter, starts/stops or live opponents. Baseline → final holdout accuracy: seed 71, 50.0% → 46.5%; seed 42719, 39.5% → 87.1%; seed 982451653, 51.2% → 74.2%. Final moving recall: 53.9%, 74.2%, 48.4%; stationary false positives: 60.9%, 0%, 0%. One seed failed to improve; these results demonstrate possible discrimination under a simplified objective, not reliable convergence or improved live hunting. An earlier raw-position input trial remained near chance, motivating the translation-centered history representation. None of these trained genomes seeds the fresh live population.

Remaining limits: live training quality and balance, latency robustness of short sampled histories, realistic start/stop discrimination, physical touch, OS reduced motion and maximum-player performance require further evaluation. The interface describes learned senses and removes promises of invisibility. The existing static fitness chart remains a task reward signal, not detection accuracy.
